# Playtesting with a child

A short guide for a grown-up watching a child (5–10) play The Good Garden for
the first time. The point is to watch, not to teach: what the child does
without help tells us what the game needs.

## Before you start

- **Windows:** unzip `GoodGarden-Windows.zip` and open `GoodGarden.exe`. The
  build isn't signed, so Windows may say "Windows protected your PC": choose
  **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
- **Mac:** unzip `GoodGarden.zip` and open **The Good Garden**. The build
  isn't notarized, so the first time, right-click (or Control-click) the app
  and choose **Open**, then **Open** again. If macOS still refuses, open
  **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and choose **Open Anyway**.
- **Tablet:** see [TABLETS.md](TABLETS.md) for Android, and for iPad (in
  Safari, or as an app built on a Mac).
- Words are read aloud with the computer's own reading voice. If the game
  says there is no reading voice, the child can still play; a grown-up can
  read the letters aloud.
- The game saves by itself. The **Pause** tile (bottom right) holds Settings,
  the grown-ups' panel (work level: younger / older), and **Save and stop
  playing**, which returns to the title. Settings are also on the title screen.
- Plan for one in-game day per sitting (about 10–20 minutes). Days 1–11 are
  ready. Resting in bed ends the day.

## While they play

Sit beside them and stay quiet unless they're upset or truly stuck for more
than a minute. If you do help, note what you said.

Jot down, for each day:

1. **Finding the way.** After reading the letter, did they know where to go?
   How long did they wander? Did they open the map?
2. **Starting work.** Did they find the grown-up or the thing to use? Did
   they understand the yes / not yet choice?
3. **The boards.** Could they drag the pieces? Where did they hesitate? Did
   they use **Show me** or **Hear it**? Did a piece slide back and confuse
   them, or did they understand why?
4. **Words.** Which words or sentences did they ask about?
5. **Manners.** Did they read or say the greetings and thank-yous? Did any
   line feel odd to them?
6. **Feelings.** When did they smile, lean in, or say something about what
   they'd made? When did they look bored, or try to leave?
7. **Free time.** Without a task, what did they do: explore, the chapel, the
   books, the river, the animals (stroking the lambs, cats, the dog, cows,
   horse and pigs, or scattering grain for the hens)?
8. **Coming back.** Next time, did they want to play, and did they remember
   what they'd planted or made?

## Afterwards

Ask, in their words:

- "What was your favourite part?"
- "Was anything tricky?"
- "Who is Miss Miriam / Mr. Tobias / Mrs. Ruth?"
- "What would you like to do next in the garden?"

Note their age, the work level used, and the device (mouse, trackpad or
touch screen). Then send the notes back so the next round of changes can
follow what children actually do.
