# Design Constitution

1. **Game first, curriculum underneath.** Children see a world and people, not subject menus.
2. **Concrete before abstract.** Let the child manipulate, observe, compare, build, count, pour, measure, sort, or retell before naming the academic concept.
3. **Discovery before explanation.** Prefer environmental prompts and natural consequences to lectures.
4. **Intrinsic before extrinsic reward.** Competence, independence, relationships, and visible world improvement are the rewards.
5. **Freedom within boundaries.** A daily invitation provides direction, while free work permits meaningful choice.
6. **Mistakes should teach.** Favor self-correcting materials and environmental feedback over red Xs.
7. **Faith is never currency.** No prayer XP, virtue points, Bible streaks, or scored devotion.
8. **Reverence matters.** Chapel/Atrium interactions are quieter and intentionally distinct from ordinary tasks.
9. **Do not gamify virtue.** Create opportunities to practice honesty, service, stewardship, patience, forgiveness, courage, gratitude, diligence, and compassion; do not reduce them to meters.
10. **The world remembers.** Planting, repairing, helping, building, and restoring should leave persistent evidence.
11. **Digital should lead back to reality.** Optional real-world invitations encourage practical life, observation, service, and family participation.
12. **Respect the child.** Calm pacing, readable interfaces, no manipulative retention mechanics, no ads, no dark patterns.
13. **Reuse systems, vary context.** Build robust manipulatives and activity primitives rather than dozens of one-off minigames.
14. **Adults observe rather than hover.** Parent/teacher tools emphasize growth and observed work, not surveillance or constant grading.
